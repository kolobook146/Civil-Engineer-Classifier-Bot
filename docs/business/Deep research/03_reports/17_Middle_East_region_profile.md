# Stage 17: Middle East Region Profile

## Purpose

This report adds the Middle East as a dedicated regional layer in the research corpus. The evidence base is built from official owner-side, megaproject, PMIS, and consultancy practice sources.

## Main Conclusion

The Middle East currently shows one of the strongest openly visible enterprise schedule-governance models in the corpus.

Its signature is:

- owner-mandated PMIS and PMDS environments;
- explicit Primavera and CPM requirements;
- cost-loaded master schedule logic;
- integration with change, payment, EDMS, KPI, and contractor governance;
- strong PMO / TPMO / project-controls capability;
- visible linkage to BIM and information-management maturity.

## Layer 1: Academic and Professional Formation

The academic layer in the region is still strongly rooted in:

- CPM
- PERT
- resource leveling
- progress measurement
- earned value

This matters because regional enterprise practice does not need to replace academic planning grammar. It extends it into a much stronger owner-side operating model.

## Layer 2: Owner-Side Enterprise Governance

### Ashghal

What stands out:

- `APMS` is presented as an enterprise operating environment, not just a reporting add-on;
- the visible control scope includes schedule, change, payment, and collaborative working;
- owner-side tender requirements explicitly call for a cost-loaded `Project Master Schedule`, `Primavera`, `Critical Path Method`, validation, document control, and change procedures.

Interpretation:

Ashghal is one of the clearest open examples of an owner that operationalizes schedule governance through PMIS, process, and contractor obligations.

### Aramco

What stands out:

- project management is framed explicitly around safe, on-time, and on-budget delivery;
- contractor capability expectations are visible;
- commissioning, startup, and large-system delivery are central;
- megaproject practice references capital management systems and large-scale controls.

Interpretation:

Aramco broadens the regional picture from PMIS governance to megaproject execution, commissioning, and enterprise contractor ecosystems.

### ADNOC

What stands out:

- one public specification explicitly treats the project schedule as the main progress-control document;
- schedule is tied to milestones, reporting, and forecasting discipline.

Interpretation:

ADNOC provides unusually direct evidence that schedule governance is specification-led and contractually operational in the region.

## Layer 3: PMO and Megaproject Controls

### Red Sea Global

What stands out:

- project-controls capability is publicly recognized as a major delivery competence;
- technical PMO and project controls are visible, not hidden back-office functions.

### Hill International regional projects

What stands out:

- `Riyadh Metro` explicitly lists project management, construction management, project controls, testing, commissioning, and PMIS;
- `Saudi Landbridge` highlights programme management, procurement management, execution planning, and project controls across phases.

Interpretation:

These sources confirm that large regional infrastructure delivery depends on full-cycle controls systems, not on schedule production alone.

## Layer 4: BIM and Information Management

`SRC-A-117` is especially important because it shows that ISO 19650 and BIM information-management capability are becoming enterprise expectations in the regional market.

Interpretation:

The Middle East profile is therefore not only PMIS-heavy. It is converging toward an integrated schedule + PMIS + BIM + controlled-information model.

## Distinctive Regional Best Practices

The strongest current best-practice signatures are:

1. cost-loaded master schedule requirements under owner governance;
2. formal use of `Primavera` and `CPM` in contractual and PMIS environments;
3. integration of schedule with change, payment, document control, and KPI reporting;
4. project-controls capability treated as a strategic PMO competence;
5. explicit attention to commissioning, handover, and completion readiness;
6. rising BIM / ISO-based information-management expectations.

## Comparison with Other Regions

### Versus USA

The Middle East is similar in its seriousness about controls, but more visibly PMIS- and owner-system-centered in the open sources reviewed here.

### Versus EU

The Middle East is less stage-gate public-policy-oriented and more operationally explicit about PMIS, Primavera, and contractual master schedules.

### Versus CIS

The Middle East is less rooted in organizational-technological documentation and more rooted in owner-controlled systems and PMIS governance.

### Versus China

The Middle East is less smart-cockpit-led than the China enterprise layer, but it is stronger in openly visible contractual and PMIS governance requirements.

## Final Assessment

The Middle East should now be treated as a dedicated comparative zone in this research. Its practice is mature, highly governed, PMIS-centered, and strongly relevant for any future target model intended for investment construction projects.
