# Regional Profile: China

## 1. Executive Summary

Researcher inference from the first-pass China evidence:

- China shows a clear dual-track model:
  - classic construction-organization and network-planning logic;
  - rapidly expanding digital-supervision and intelligent-construction logic.
- Construction schedule remains tightly coupled to organization design.
- Delivery scheduling extends strongly into feasibility, approvals, procurement, and public-governance sequence.
- The digital layer is not replacing the classic layer; it is being layered on top of it.

## 2. Dominant Regional Grammar

The recurring China grammar is built around:

- construction project management standards;
- construction organization design;
- engineering network planning;
- feasibility-study and approval logic;
- digital housing and urban-rural development;
- intelligent construction;
- digital supervision archives and online oversight.

This gives China a wider combined grammar than a purely production-centered school.

## 3. What Counts as the Main Schedule Artifact

China appears to treat schedule logic through several interacting artifact classes:

- project-management standard and management plan;
- construction organization design package;
- network plan;
- feasibility-study and approval timeline;
- digital supervision records and platform-based checkpoints;
- intelligent-construction process artifacts.

This is wider than a single "schedule file" concept.

## 4. Type of Schedule Most Developed in the Regional School

Two strong layers are visible:

### 4.1 Classic production-oriented layer

- construction organization schedule;
- network plan;
- execution sequence and organization logic;
- site and method coordination.

### 4.2 Delivery and governance layer

- feasibility and project-approval schedule;
- procurement and package sequence;
- lifecycle supervision checkpoints;
- digital process monitoring.

## 5. Levels of Detail

The first-pass evidence suggests several detail mechanisms:

- project-management standard layer;
- organization-design hierarchy;
- network-method technical layer;
- digital checkpoint / archive layer;
- lifecycle phase and approval layer.

Researcher inference: China may require a multi-layer LOD model where classical engineering decomposition and digital-governance checkpoints coexist.

## 6. Cross-Project Processes Inside the Schedule

China strongly includes:

- feasibility;
- approval;
- management planning;
- procurement and package logic;
- construction organization;
- supervision;
- digital archive management;
- intelligent-construction process integration.

This makes China particularly important for understanding how delivery and oversight processes live inside the schedule model.

## 7. Characteristic Entities and Artifacts

Most characteristic entities so far:

- construction organization design
- engineering network plan
- project-management plan
- feasibility-study milestone
- approval gate
- digital supervision archive
- intelligent-construction practice package
- progress-warning object

## 8. Strengths of the China Layer

- Strong integration of construction planning with national standardization.
- Strong linkage between project realization and public-governance sequence.
- Clear momentum toward digitized oversight and intelligent-construction workflows.
- Wider lifecycle framing than a pure site-execution schedule.

## 9. Limitations or Gaps in the Accessible Source Base

- Public access to authoritative norms is uneven.
- Secondary-access repositories are sometimes needed for technical planning standards.
- Enterprise scheduling software and field update practice require a later dedicated pass.

## 10. Research Implication

China should be modeled as a combination of:

- classical engineering and organizational planning;
- delivery-governance sequencing;
- digital oversight and intelligent-construction data objects.

Reducing China to only network planning or only digital platforms would be wrong.
