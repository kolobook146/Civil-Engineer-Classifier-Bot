# Taxonomy of Schedule Types

## Purpose

This document creates the first taxonomy of schedule types while preserving the required distinction between:

- schedule type;
- application domain;
- management level;
- planning horizon.

The same schedule artifact can occupy more than one axis at the same time.

## 1. The Four Main Axes

### 1.1 Schedule type

What kind of schedule artifact it is.

### 1.2 Application domain

What problem the schedule is used to manage.

### 1.3 Management level

Who uses it and at what decision altitude.

### 1.4 Planning horizon

How far forward and at what refresh frequency it is intended to operate.

## 3. Initial Definitions

### 3.1 Integrated master schedule

A higher-order schedule or synchronized schedule view that connects major project streams, often across delivery functions rather than only site work.

### 3.2 Master schedule

A high-level controlling schedule used for major dates, key interfaces, and management reporting.

### 3.3 Milestone schedule

A reduced schedule centered on key control dates, approvals, contractual events, and executive reporting.

### 3.4 Phase schedule

A schedule focused on one major delivery phase, such as design, procurement, construction, or commissioning.

### 3.5 Detailed control schedule

A decomposed schedule used for active control, variance analysis, progress updates, and forecasting.

### 3.6 Detailed construction schedule

A production-centered detailed schedule used for physical execution control on site.

### 3.7 Engineering schedule

A schedule focused on design packages, deliverables, review cycles, and discipline coordination.

### 3.8 Procurement schedule

A schedule focused on packages, bids, evaluations, awards, submittals, fabrication, shipping, and delivery.

### 3.9 Commissioning schedule

A schedule focused on testing, system readiness, punch items, startup, and acceptance sequence.

### 3.10 Contract schedule

A schedule artifact required, reviewed, approved, or governed by contract.

### 3.11 Recovery schedule

A corrective schedule prepared after slippage, disruption, or changed execution strategy.

### 3.12 Update schedule

A periodic revision or status-bearing schedule used to report current progress and forecast.

### 3.13 Look-ahead schedule

A rolling short-term schedule that translates the control schedule into executable near-term sequence.

### 3.14 Short-interval schedule

A very near-term schedule for daily or weekly work coordination, often crew- or workfront-oriented.

### 3.15 Project delivery schedule

A lifecycle schedule covering pre-construction, development, approvals, procurement, construction, and closeout.

### 3.16 Investment / development schedule

A front-end project realization schedule emphasizing feasibility, approvals, financing, and development gates before full execution.

## 4. Application Domains

The same schedule types may be used in different domains:

- production control;
- project delivery control;
- owner oversight;
- contract administration;
- procurement coordination;
- engineering management;
- claims or delay analysis;
- executive reporting;
- PMO / project controls governance.

## 5. Management Levels

The first-pass evidence suggests at least these levels:

- executive / sponsor;
- program / portfolio;
- project management;
- contract administration;
- project controls;
- site management;
- workfront / superintendent;
- crew or short-interval coordination.

## 6. Planning Horizons

The first-pass horizon model is:

- lifecycle / long-range;
- phase-level;
- detailed control;
- rolling wave;
- look-ahead;
- short interval.

## 7. Important Research Rule

Do not confuse a schedule type with a use case.

Examples:

- A contract schedule may be a master schedule or a detailed control schedule.
- A look-ahead schedule is usually a planning-horizon choice, not a complete substitute for the control schedule.
- A milestone schedule may be used for executive reporting, contract control, or financing review.
