# International Best Practices Overview

## Purpose

This report summarizes the first-pass international best-practice layer outside strict regional profiling. It focuses on professional institutes, public-owner governance, contract families, and multilateral institutional practice.

Important note: this is an initial synthesis and not the final comparative report.

## 1. Best-Practice Clusters Identified So Far

### 1.1 US-curated professional and owner-side controls

Main source cluster:

- PMI
- AACE International
- USACE
- FTA
- GSA
- Caltrans

Observed best-practice themes:

- formal schedule model terminology;
- explicit schedule governance procedures;
- schedule review for completeness and reliability;
- baseline and update discipline;
- specification-driven owner requirements;
- differentiated rigor by project type or risk;
- integration of schedule control with broader project controls.

This cluster is strongly CPM- and controls-oriented.

### 1.2 UK / Europe-curated professional practice

Main source cluster:

- APM
- CIOB
- FIDIC

Observed best-practice themes:

- maturity of scheduling capability as a management system;
- dynamic management of time in major projects;
- strong linkage between time management and collaboration;
- broad project-delivery framing through procurement and contract guidance;
- emphasis on formal contract structure and disciplined administration.

Compared with the US controls cluster, this group currently appears somewhat more governance- and delivery-oriented and somewhat less explicitly software-centric in the first pass.

### 1.3 Asia-curated institutional and China-curated planning layer

Main source cluster:

- ADB
- China NDRC
- China SAMR-related standard-system material

Observed best-practice themes:

- contract monitoring and time-extension control within multilateral delivery governance;
- strong pre-construction emphasis on feasibility, approvals, and front-end project structuring;
- project administration and document control appearing close to scheduling concerns;
- lifecycle framing before physical construction begins.

This is only the initial Asia layer. China requires a much deeper dedicated pass later.

## 2. Best-Practice Themes That Repeat Across Curator Zones

Researcher inference: the following themes appear cross-zone in the first pass.

- Schedule quality depends on governed structure, not only on tool usage.
- Schedule control needs explicit status and forecast discipline.
- A baseline without review and update procedures is not enough.
- Major projects need more than one schedule view or level.
- Time management spills into procurement, contracts, approvals, and change control.
- Owner-side review criteria materially shape what counts as an acceptable schedule.

## 3. Best-Practice Themes That Are Strong but Not Yet Proven Universal

These are strong patterns, but the evidence is not yet sufficient to call them universal:

- maturity models as a standard scheduling practice;
- dynamic time model terminology;
- tiered schedule rigor policies;
- explicit schedule basis documents in all jurisdictions;
- multi-baseline usage in field practice outside software capability.

## 5. What This Means for the Research Program

The first-pass international layer already shows that the research program should not treat scheduling as only:

- CPM math;
- construction bar charts;
- software fields;
- site production sequence.

Instead, the research must keep four interacting layers visible:

- schedule data model;
- schedule governance model;
- delivery lifecycle model;
- contract and oversight model.
