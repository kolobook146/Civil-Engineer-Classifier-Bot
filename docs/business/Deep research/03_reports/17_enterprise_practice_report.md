# Stage 17: Enterprise Practice Report

## Scope and Method

Stage 17 focuses on enterprise practice: owner requirements, PMO and PMIS operating models, EPC execution systems, megaproject delivery controls, and major consultancy project-controls practice. The objective is to understand how scheduling is actually governed in live project systems.

The practice base is organized across five zones:

1. USA
2. CIS
3. EU
4. China
5. Middle East

## Main Conclusion

Enterprise practice is where schedule management stops being only a planning method and becomes an operating system.

Across the source base, the schedule is rarely treated as a lone planning file. It is embedded in:

- PMIS and project-controls offices;
- cost, change, payment, and document-control workflows;
- handover and readiness systems;
- dashboards, KPI cycles, and performance reviews;
- smart-site and digital-cockpit platforms.

## Enterprise Practice Archetypes

### 1. Owner-side enterprise governance

Representative sources:

- `SRC-A-085` to `SRC-A-089`
- `SRC-A-105` to `SRC-A-113`

Signature:

- schedule is mandated, reviewed, assessed, and maintained as an enterprise governance object;
- owner systems define structure, frequency, accountability, and integration with cost and change;
- PMIS is often the real operating environment.

### 2. EPC and integrator execution systems

Representative sources:

- `SRC-A-093`
- `SRC-A-101`
- `SRC-A-109`

Signature:

- schedule is linked to work packages, delivery packages, commissioning, and execution readiness;
- handover and startup become explicit control domains;
- digital delivery increasingly sits inside the execution model.

### 3. Consultancy-led project-controls systems

Representative sources:

- `SRC-A-090`
- `SRC-A-091`
- `SRC-A-092`
- `SRC-A-115`
- `SRC-A-116`

Signature:

- controls practice is sold and organized as an integrated service;
- schedule, cost, risk, reporting, and digital reporting are treated as one performance system;
- single baseline and integrated controls language is explicit.

### 4. Smart construction and digital-governance platforms

Representative sources:

- `SRC-A-099`
- `SRC-A-102`
- `SRC-A-103`
- `SRC-A-104`
- `SRC-A-114`

Signature:

- progress visibility is increasingly mediated through smart-site, cockpit, and platform workflows;
- the schedule becomes linked to issues, locations, inspections, and digital records;
- platform state matters almost as much as schedule logic.

## Zone-by-Zone Analysis

### USA

Main sources:

- `SRC-A-085`, `SRC-A-086`, `SRC-A-087`
- `SRC-A-088`, `SRC-A-089`
- `SRC-A-090`, `SRC-A-091`, `SRC-A-092`
- `SRC-A-093`

Practice signature:

- the U.S. remains the strongest zone for formal schedule-governance discipline;
- owner-side practice is handbook-driven and assessment-driven;
- enterprise controls integrate schedule with cost, risk, reporting, and PMO structures;
- EPC practice extends this toward digital work packages and delivery execution.

What is distinctive:

- schedule capability itself is managed as an enterprise discipline;
- integrated controls language is explicit and mature;
- reporting, quality assessment, and stewardship are formalized rather than left to planner habit.

### CIS

Main sources:

- `SRC-A-099`
- `SRC-A-100`
- `SRC-A-101`

Practice signature:

- enterprise practice adds a much stronger digital-control layer than the earlier academic/normative seed suggested;
- BIM-enabled process coverage and real-time control systems coexist with classical organizational-technological foundations;
- major-enterprise and strategic-sector delivery still shape practice.

What is distinctive:

- schedule logic is still close to construction organization and delivery execution;
- digital control is often introduced through enterprise transformation and smart-site supervision rather than through one global PMO handbook.

### EU

Main sources:

- `SRC-A-094`
- `SRC-A-095`
- `SRC-A-096`
- `SRC-A-097`
- `SRC-A-098`

Practice signature:

- the EU enterprise layer is strongest on programme organization, handbook-led planning systems, governance, and handover strategy;
- schedule practice is deeply connected to programme integration and transition readiness;
- quality and controlled-document obligations sit close to the delivery system.

What is distinctive:

- completion, handover, and governance are more visibly formalized than in many academic scheduling treatments;
- the planning handbook appears as a key enterprise artefact.

### China

Main sources:

- `SRC-A-102`
- `SRC-A-103`
- `SRC-A-104`

Practice signature:

- enterprise practice is strongly platformized;
- smart construction is expressed through cockpits, smart-site platforms, and digital innovation systems;
- the practice layer operationalizes the digital-governance direction seen earlier in MOHURD sources.

What is distinctive:

- platform and cockpit objects become central enterprise control mechanisms;
- the enterprise layer is less about one published scheduling handbook and more about smart-site operational systems.

### Middle East

Main sources:

- `SRC-A-105` to `SRC-A-117`

Practice signature:

- the Middle East is the strongest region in the current corpus for explicit owner-mandated PMIS and schedule-governance requirements;
- PMIS, Primavera, cost-loaded master schedules, change control, payment control, and document governance are tightly coupled;
- PMO and technical PMO capability is treated as a strategic delivery asset on megaprojects.

What is distinctive:

- owner requirements are unusually explicit and operational;
- handover, commissioning, and contractor capability requirements are visible in delivery practice;
- BIM and information management are becoming enterprise expectations, not optional enhancements.

## Why Middle East Deserves a Separate Zone

The Middle East layer is not just "international practice applied locally." It has a distinct signature:

- strong owner-side control systems;
- strong PMIS and PMDS operating environments;
- explicit Primavera and CPM requirement patterns;
- structured change, payment, KPI, and EDMS integration;
- megaproject-scale PMO and TPMO operating models;
- fast convergence between schedule governance, PMIS, BIM, and delivery reporting.

This makes Middle East practice materially different from a simplified extension of either U.S. or EU norms.

## Enterprise Practice Compared with Academic Formalization

### What enterprise keeps from academia

- CPM and network logic;
- baseline, update, progress, and control mathematics;
- resource and performance logic.

### What enterprise adds

- PMIS as the real control environment;
- PMO, controls office, and recurring review cycles;
- document governance, EDMS, approval, and payment integration;
- completion-readiness, transition, and handover governance;
- linked digital platforms, dashboards, and KPI obligations.

## Enterprise Practice Compared with Software

### Strong matches

- `Primavera P6`, `Primavera Cloud`, and `Deltek` align strongly with U.S. and some Middle East owner/PMO practice.
- `Unifier` aligns strongly with document-system and governed-container enterprise environments.
- `Autodesk Build`, `Procore`, `Glodon`, and `CSCEC` platform practice align strongly with smart-site and linked-object enterprise control.

### Important mismatch

Enterprise practice usually demands more operating-model governance than software documentation alone reveals. Software exposes objects and fields; enterprise practice defines who owns them, how often they are reviewed, and how they affect approvals, payments, readiness, and escalation.

## Result

Stage 17 shows that the real-world schedule model is enterprise-operated. This is the strongest argument so far against reducing the future business model to tasks, dates, dependencies, and baselines only.
