# Regional Profile: USA

## 1. Executive Summary

Researcher inference from the first-pass U.S. evidence:

- The U.S. has the strongest explicit schedule-governance and schedule-quality-assurance culture in the current research corpus.
- The regional center of gravity is CPM- and IMS-oriented, but not only mathematically; it is also procedurally governed.
- Owner-side review, acceptance, statusing, and schedule-health criteria are highly developed.
- The U.S. profile integrates delivery and construction through formal schedule management rather than leaving them as loosely connected artifacts.

## 2. Dominant Regional Grammar

The recurring U.S. grammar is built around:

- CPM logic;
- integrated master schedule;
- baseline and status date discipline;
- schedule quality assessment;
- contractor submittal and owner review;
- update narratives and review meetings;
- schedule specification;
- project controls integration.

Compared with the CIS school, the U.S. school isolates the schedule as a more autonomous control system.

## 3. What Counts as the Main Schedule Artifact

In the U.S. source base, the schedule is often:

- a contractually governed schedule submission;
- an integrated master schedule;
- a reviewed and assessable control document;
- a baseline-backed predictive-control instrument.

It is frequently accompanied by:

- narrative reports;
- schedule specifications;
- review criteria;
- variance and forecast logic.

## 4. Type of Schedule Most Developed in the Regional School

The most developed visible types are:

- integrated master schedule;
- contract schedule;
- detailed control schedule;
- oversight review schedule;
- update schedule.

This school is strong both in project delivery and in construction control because owner-side public guidance links them through formal schedule governance.

## 5. Levels of Detail

The U.S. evidence base explicitly supports:

- schedule rigor tiers;
- detailed review criteria;
- multi-level or integrated schedule structures;
- differentiated oversight depending on project complexity or risk.

Examples in the current source base include:

- GAO best-practice assessment;
- FTA schedule review;
- Caltrans Level 1 vs Level 2 CPM requirements.

## 6. Cross-Project Processes Inside the Schedule

The U.S. layer strongly includes:

- design and review events;
- procurement and contract milestones;
- owner acceptance cycles;
- schedule updates and status cycles;
- change and recovery logic;
- major delivery interfaces beyond site execution only.

## 7. Characteristic Entities and Artifacts

Most characteristic entities so far:

- integrated master schedule
- schedule specification
- schedule quality criteria
- baseline set
- status date
- narrative update package
- review checklist
- level-of-rigor designation

## 8. Strengths of the U.S. School

- Strongest explicit schedule quality and traceability discipline.
- Strong owner-contractor governance and review logic.
- Strong integration between schedule control and broader project controls.
- Strongly developed oversight and acceptance procedures.

## 9. Limitations or Trade-offs

- Governance burden can become heavy.
- Formal compliance can overshadow field usability if overextended.
- The school may underrepresent locally adapted production-planning forms that are not expressed as formal CPM governance.

## 10. Research Implication

The U.S. region is the strongest anchor for rules about schedule quality, governance, review, baseline control, and integrated control structures. It should heavily inform the rule catalog, but it should not be mistaken for the only valid grammar of construction scheduling.
