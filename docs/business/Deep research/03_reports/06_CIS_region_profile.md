# Regional Profile: CIS

## Scope Note

This profile represents the CIS scheduling school primarily through the Russian normative and academic core, with supporting evidence from the broader post-Soviet academic tradition. This reflects source availability as much as regional reality.

## 1. Executive Summary

Researcher inference from the first-pass CIS evidence:

- The regional center of gravity is organizational-technological planning.
- Scheduling is strongly embedded in document systems such as POS and PPR.
- Production planning is more explicit and formalized than broad investment-delivery scheduling.
- Linear, calendar, and network representations coexist rather than being replaced by one dominant format.

## 2. Dominant Regional Grammar

The recurring CIS grammar is built around:

- `organization of construction`;
- `organizational-technological documentation`;
- `calendar planning`;
- `network planning`;
- `construction organization project (POS)`;
- `work execution plan (PPR)`;
- `flow / line-of-balance / cyclogram thinking`;
- strong linkage between schedule, site organization, and resource deployment.

This differs from the U.S. tendency to isolate the schedule as a more autonomous enterprise-control artifact.

## 3. What Counts as the Main Schedule Artifact

In the CIS school, the schedule is often not only:

- an activity list;
- a CPM file;
- an owner-reviewed contract schedule.

It is more often a component of a larger planning package containing:

- preparatory-period logic;
- object and work-package calendar plans;
- work methods;
- construction sequence;
- temporary facilities and site logistics;
- resource needs;
- brigade and machine planning.

## 4. Type of Schedule Most Developed in the Regional School

The best-developed layer in the accessible CIS evidence is the production schedule.

Strongly represented:

- object calendar plan;
- work-type calendar plan;
- network schedule;
- line or linear schedule;
- cyclogram;
- weekly-daily execution schedule.

Less explicit in the first pass:

- fully integrated investment-project delivery schedules spanning feasibility, finance, procurement, and commissioning in one dominant regional grammar.

## 5. Levels of Detail

The regional logic appears to decompose detail through:

- construction complex or project level;
- object level;
- work-package or process level;
- short-interval execution level.

Researcher inference: the CIS school expresses level of detail more through planning-document hierarchy and work decomposition than through a universal L1-L4 vocabulary.

## 6. Cross-Project Processes Inside the Schedule

Cross-project processes are present, but the center of gravity remains closer to construction organization than to broad delivery governance.

Visible or implied in the first-pass evidence:

- preparatory period;
- organizational preparation;
- supply and resource preparation;
- technological sequence;
- execution method selection.

Less explicit than in U.S. or EU public-governance sources:

- contract-event governance;
- financing milestones;
- structured owner-side review and schedule acceptance;
- formal integrated master schedule logic.

## 7. Characteristic Entities and Artifacts

Most characteristic regional entities so far:

- POS
- PPR
- calendar plan
- network graph
- linear graph / cyclogram
- preparatory period
- brigade / machine deployment
- site organization elements

## 8. Strengths of the CIS School

- Strong operational tie between schedule and actual production organization.
- Strong sensitivity to work sequence, site conditions, and execution technology.
- Rich vocabulary for production decomposition and workfront organization.
- Good compatibility with crew-, machine-, and process-based field planning.

## 9. Limitations or Gaps in the Accessible Source Base

- Publicly accessible modern owner-side schedule governance is less visible than in the U.S.
- End-to-end delivery scheduling is less clearly standardized in the accessible materials than production scheduling.
- Access to authoritative norms often depends on mirrored or institutional-hosted copies.

## 10. Research Implication

The CIS region should not be modeled as "just another CPM variant." Its native planning grammar is organizational-technological and document-system-oriented. If the future business model ignores POS/PPR-like structures, it will miss much of the regional logic.
