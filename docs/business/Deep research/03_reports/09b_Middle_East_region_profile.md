# Regional Profile: Middle East

## Scope Note

This profile represents the Middle East scheduling school through a combination of:

- academic formalization in Egypt, Saudi Arabia, and Qatar;
- owner-side and authority-side governance in Qatar, Saudi Arabia, Abu Dhabi, and Dubai;
- megaproject and consultancy practice in Saudi Arabia and the wider Gulf.

The accessible source base is therefore weighted toward the Gulf and formal institutional practice more than toward informal contractor-only production methods.

## 1. Executive Summary

Researcher inference from the current Middle East evidence:

- The regional center of gravity is not pure production scheduling, but owner-governed project delivery and enterprise controls.
- Classical CPM/PERT teaching remains strong and regionally visible.
- The schedule is often embedded in PMIS, PMDS, or authority-run digital systems rather than treated as a standalone planning file.
- Cost-loaded master schedules, Primavera requirements, change/payment/document control, and handover governance are unusually explicit in the open source base.
- A distinctive authority-governance layer is visible through qualification, classification, performance evaluation, and digital permitting systems.

## 2. Dominant Regional Grammar

The recurring Middle East grammar is built around:

- CPM / PERT and control-method teaching;
- cost-loaded `Project Master Schedule`;
- Primavera-centered owner requirements;
- PMIS / PMDS operating environments;
- change, payment, EDMS, and KPI integration;
- commissioning, startup, and handover governance;
- engineering-firm qualification and authority-led performance evaluation;
- digital permitting and future-facing digital-governance discourse.

This is different from:

- the CIS organizational-technological document grammar;
- the EU public-cycle and procurement-governance grammar;
- the China smart-site and classic-organization dual-track grammar.

It is closest to the U.S. controls school, but more visibly owner-system- and PMIS-centered in the accessible sources.

## 3. What Counts as the Main Schedule Artifact

In the Middle East layer, the schedule is often not only:

- a CPM file;
- a contractor schedule;
- a planning course output.

It more often appears as part of a governed package containing:

- project master schedule;
- cost-loaded baseline or validated master programme;
- PMIS / PMDS record set;
- change and payment workflows;
- document-control and EDMS obligations;
- completion and commissioning controls;
- authority-side performance and classification records.

Researcher inference: the regional artifact model is strongly system-centered and owner-governed.

## 4. Type of Schedule Most Developed in the Regional School

The best-developed layer in the accessible Middle East evidence is the project delivery and owner-control schedule.

Strongly represented:

- project master schedule;
- cost-loaded baseline schedule;
- programme or portfolio schedule inside PMIS / PMDS;
- project controls and progress-control schedule;
- handover and commissioning schedule logic;
- megaproject corridor or programme controls.

Also visible:

- classical CPM and network methods in academic teaching;
- contractor and consultant performance tracking tied to delivery governance.

Less visible in the current open source base:

- line-of-balance or cyclogram-style regional production-school artifacts comparable to CIS;
- one highly standardized public project-cycle grammar comparable to the EU institutional layer.

## 5. Levels of Detail

The regional logic appears to decompose detail through:

- portfolio or programme layer;
- project layer;
- contract or package layer;
- master schedule and baseline layer;
- completion, commissioning, and handover layer;
- authority and performance-evaluation layer;
- permit and digital workflow layer.

Researcher inference: Middle East detail logic is often expressed through enterprise-control hierarchy and authority workflow, not only through work decomposition.

## 6. Cross-Project Processes Inside the Schedule

The Middle East layer strongly includes:

- contractor and consultant governance;
- change management;
- payment management;
- document-control and EDMS integration;
- completion certification;
- commissioning and startup;
- handover and readiness;
- firm classification and qualification context;
- digital permit and authority workflow interaction;
- KPI and performance-review cycles.

This makes Middle East particularly important for understanding how schedule logic lives inside enterprise and institutional delivery systems.

## 7. Characteristic Entities and Artifacts

Most characteristic entities and artifacts so far:

- `Project Master Schedule`
- PMIS / PMDS operating environment
- cost-loaded baseline schedule
- change and payment workflow
- completion certificate / owner performance-evaluation record
- engineering-firm classification record
- professional qualification / engineer grade record
- digital permit-platform project record
- technical PMO / project-controls office
- commissioning and handover plan

## 8. Strengths of the Middle East Layer

- Strong visibility of owner-side governance and delivery requirements.
- Strong coupling between schedule, cost, payment, change, and document control.
- Strong maturity of PMIS-, PMDS-, and PMO-driven delivery systems in the accessible source base.
- Clear attention to commissioning, startup, and handover.
- Emerging integration of BIM, ISO 19650, digital twins, and digital authority platforms.
- Strong academic continuity of CPM/control logic supporting enterprise practice.

## 9. Limitations or Gaps in the Accessible Source Base

- Open sources are weighted toward owners, authorities, and large consultancies more than toward contractor-internal production-planning methods.
- The evidence base is Gulf-heavy; it likely does not fully represent the whole region.
- Full technical specs and operating manuals are not always public even where summary requirements are visible.
- Less direct evidence is available on purely site-level production scheduling than on owner- and PMIS-level governance.

## 10. Research Implication

The Middle East region should not be modeled as:

- merely imported U.S. project controls;
- merely generic international practice;
- merely megaproject branding plus software.

Its native accessible grammar is:

- classical control-method education;
- explicit owner-governed PMIS and master schedule requirements;
- authority-led qualification and performance systems;
- strong commissioning and completion governance;
- increasing digital-governance and permitting integration.

If the future business model ignores PMIS, master schedules, completion records, classification systems, and authority workflows, it will miss a large part of the regional logic.
