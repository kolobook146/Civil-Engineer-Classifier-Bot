# Narrative Report

## Purpose

This narrative report is the Stage 20 assembled reading path across the research corpus. It summarizes the highest-signal findings without replacing the stage-level reports.

## 1. Research Contour

The research investigated construction and investment-project scheduling without premature narrowing to a pilot solution. The study separated:

- universal principles vs regional specifics;
- schedule type vs application domain;
- production schedule vs project delivery schedule;
- academic formalization vs enterprise practice vs software-native data structures.

## 2. Universal Core

The durable global core remains:

- tasks and milestones;
- dependency logic;
- calendars and schedule math;
- baselines, actuals, and progress control;
- hierarchy and decomposition;
- statusing and reporting discipline.

This core is necessary but not sufficient.

## 3. Schedule Types and Two-Contour Model

The corpus confirms two overlapping but different schedule universes:

- `production schedule`: site execution, work sequence, crews, resources, short-interval control;
- `project delivery schedule`: approvals, procurement, contracts, financing, handover, readiness, PMIS checkpoints.

The research repeatedly showed that these should not be collapsed into one flat model.

## 4. Five-Region Picture

The current regional synthesis covers:

- `CIS`: strongest in organizational-technological and document-system production planning.
- `EU`: strongest in stage-gate, procurement, project-cycle, and handover governance.
- `USA`: strongest in schedule quality, owner governance, and integrated controls.
- `China`: strongest in classic planning plus digital-governance and smart-site integration.
- `Middle East`: strongest in owner-governed PMIS, master schedule control, and authority-linked delivery governance.

## 5. Five Confirmed Families

The corpus now supports five confirmed modeling families:

1. `decomposition`
2. `document-system`
3. `stage-gate`
4. `report/log`
5. `digital-governance`

The main research shift after Stage 10-19 is that these families are not theoretical abstractions. They recur in regional practice, institute methods, enterprise systems, and software documentation.

## 6. Academic -> Enterprise -> Software Progression

### Academic formalization

Defines the mental grammar:

- CPM and controls in USA and Middle East;
- organizational-technological planning in CIS;
- programme and governance orientation in EU;
- construction organization plus intelligent-construction framing in China.

### Enterprise practice

Defines the operating grammar:

- PMIS and PMO systems;
- master schedules and controls cycles;
- cost, change, payment, and document governance;
- handover and readiness systems;
- dashboards, KPIs, qualification, and performance governance.

### Software-native data structures

Define the executable grammar:

- tasks, WBS, baselines, and codes;
- schedule sheets and configured fields;
- version history and audit trails;
- linked objects and digital records;
- smart-site and platform state.

## 7. Main Comparative Result

No single region, school, or product can serve as the whole target-model template.

- USA and Middle East are strongest anchors for governance and enterprise controls.
- CIS and China prevent the model from collapsing into a task-only CPM file.
- EU is critical for project-delivery governance and handover logic.
- software confirms that the schedule system is a hybrid object environment rather than a simple activity table.

## 8. What the Research Package Now Enables

The corpus is now ready for:

- source-backed rule-catalog drafting;
- target-model selection and design in Stage 21;
- later documentation transfer into project business docs after model selection.
